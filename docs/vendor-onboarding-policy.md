# Blueprint Home Solutions Vendor Onboarding Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how Blueprint Home Solutions should onboard contractors, tradesmen, and other home service providers while the company is still small and operating with limited staff capacity.

The goal is not to build a large-company compliance department on day one. The goal is to:

- reduce obvious fraud and poor-fit vendors
- create a repeatable manual process
- protect homeowners during early growth
- allow Blueprint to move faster without making promises it cannot operationally support

## Operating Assumption

Blueprint is a small company and vendor volume may be slow at first. The onboarding policy should therefore be:

- manual first
- lightweight but defensible
- stricter for lead access than for simple account creation
- easy to tighten later if promotion volume increases

## Vendor Status Levels

### 1. Account Created

The vendor has signed up but has not been reviewed by Blueprint.

What this means:

- can create an account
- cannot be treated as verified
- should not receive homeowner introductions
- should not receive public trust messaging that implies approval

### 2. Pending Review

The vendor has submitted the information Blueprint needs for manual review.

Required items:

- legal name or business name
- email
- phone number
- trade or service category
- service area
- license number if applicable
- profile photo or business identity information if available

### 3. Verified Limited Access

The vendor has passed a basic manual review and may receive limited lead visibility.

Required checks:

- identity appears real and consistent
- license checked if the trade requires one
- business/contact details are internally consistent
- no obvious fraud signals
- admin has approved the account

What this means:

- may view sanitized leads if subscription and product rules allow
- may request introductions through Blueprint
- may not receive unrestricted homeowner contact details automatically

### 4. Trusted Active Vendor

The vendor has completed at least one real introduction flow successfully or has otherwise demonstrated reliability.

Typical signals:

- responsive communication
- no homeowner complaints
- no policy circumvention
- accurate service category
- successful admin-reviewed introduction

What this means:

- can remain verified
- can be prioritized operationally
- can be reevaluated if quality or conduct drops

## Minimum Verification Standard

Blueprint should use the following minimum standard before allowing a vendor into the homeowner lead workflow.

### For All Vendors

- valid email address
- valid phone number
- trade selection
- service area
- manual admin review

### For Licensed Trades

When a trade normally requires a license, Blueprint should collect and review:

- license number
- state or city issuing authority when relevant
- visible license status if it can be checked

If a license cannot be confirmed, the vendor should remain unverified.

### For Tradesmen or Non-Licensed Categories

If no formal license is required, Blueprint should collect at least:

- stated trade specialty
- service examples, work history, or portfolio material when available
- any available business or identity details that reduce fraud risk

## Early-Stage Manual Review Process

This is the recommended process while the company is still small.

1. Vendor signs up.
2. Vendor record is marked `Pending Review`.
3. Admin checks profile completeness.
4. Admin checks license if applicable.
5. Admin decides `Verified` or `Needs Follow-up`.
6. Verified vendor receives access according to platform rules.

If something is unclear:

- do not reject automatically unless fraud is obvious
- request clarification
- keep the account out of the lead marketplace until reviewed

## Lead Access Policy

A vendor should not receive homeowner lead access merely because they created an account.

Lead marketplace access should require:

- contractor role
- verified status
- valid subscription level or trial access

Homeowner direct contact information should stay hidden until Blueprint intentionally approves an introduction.

## Introduction Policy

Blueprint should act as the first control point between the homeowner and the vendor.

Recommended rule:

- vendor sees sanitized lead
- vendor requests introduction through Blueprint
- admin reviews the request
- Blueprint contacts homeowner
- Blueprint confirms interest and fit
- Blueprint sends the introduction

This reduces bad matches and gives the company time to operate carefully while volume is low.

## Communication Policy

Because Blueprint is small, communication must be simple and consistent.

### Immediate Automated Messages

As soon as the workflow supports it, Blueprint should send:

- homeowner acknowledgment after lead submission
- vendor acknowledgment after introduction request
- homeowner update when a vendor request is under review
- vendor update when introduction is approved, declined, or still pending

### Manual Admin Follow-Up

Until messaging is fully automated, admin should follow a simple script:

- confirm lead was received
- confirm the request is being reviewed
- confirm whether an introduction is approved
- document the outcome in admin notes or status history

## Recommended Status Model

Blueprint should use a clear status model for contractor introduction requests.

Suggested statuses:

- `Requested`
- `Admin Reviewing`
- `Homeowner Contact Pending`
- `Homeowner Confirmed`
- `Introduction Approved`
- `Declined`
- `Closed`

This gives the team a documented process even when only one person is operating it.

## Photos and Evidence

Photos are useful but should not block the basic lead workflow.

Policy:

- homeowner lead creation must work even when no photos are attached
- photos should remain optional during early rollout
- if photos fail, the lead should still submit and admin should be told photos were not included

## Risk Controls For Early Growth

Blueprint should keep these controls in place during early growth:

- no automatic exposure of homeowner direct contact data to vendors
- no vendor marked verified without manual admin review
- no assumption that signup equals approval
- no claim that Blueprint guarantees workmanship
- clear internal record of who approved a vendor and when

## When To Tighten The Policy

The policy should become stricter when any of the following happen:

- sustained weekly lead volume
- multiple vendors onboarding per week
- homeowner complaints
- evidence of fake vendors or poor matches
- increased advertising or flyer distribution

At that point Blueprint should consider:

- insurance collection
- stronger identity checks
- required portfolio examples
- probation periods
- disqualification rules
- audit logging of admin actions

## Practical Recommendation Right Now

For the current stage of the business, the right standard is:

- let vendors sign up easily
- do not let them into homeowner lead access automatically
- verify them manually before marketplace access
- keep homeowner contact details private until admin approves an introduction
- use simple acknowledgment messages and a documented review process

This is a credible policy for a small company without overbuilding too early.
